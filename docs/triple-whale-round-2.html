10 likely questions

1. What would your first 30/60/90 days look like?

First 30: instrument. Define the event taxonomy for growth surfaces. Add posthog.capture() calls to the React app for gate views, upgrade CTA clicks, plan changes. Enrich the posthog.group('shop', shopId, {...}) call with plan tier, connected platforms, and pixel status from the tw-subscription and tw-shop globals. Ship the first PostHog experiment on a single gate variant.

Next 30: measure. Build the free→Foundation funnel in PostHog: signup_completed → pixel_installed → ad_platform_connected → gate_shown → upgrade_cta_clicked → plan_upgraded. Identify the highest drop-off step. Stand up the PQL scoring service (Node.js scheduled job, ClickHouse aggregation, MongoDB write-back, PostHog group property sync).

Next 30: experiment. Run 2-3 experiments on the highest-impact gate surface. First candidate: the attribution report, showing first/last-click with a blurred multi-touch comparison. Measure conversion lift to Foundation within 7 and 14 days.

2. How would you identify the highest-impact growth lever for free→Foundation?

The attribution ceiling. Free users have the Triple Pixel collecting multi-touch data but can only view first/last-click. As ad spend grows across multiple channels, single-touch attribution becomes visibly inadequate because Google and Meta both claim credit for the same sale. The gap between what they see and what multi-touch would show them is a natural, self-evident upgrade trigger. The growth engineer's job is making that moment visible (blurred multi-touch preview, side-by-side comparison showing channel credit double-counting) and the upgrade path frictionless (Stripe Checkout, one click from the gate).

3. How would you decide what to experiment on first?

Can't decide without data. PostHog is loaded but autocapture is off and no growth events exist. First priority: instrument the free→Foundation funnel to see where users drop off. The first experiment should target the single highest-drop step with a simple A/B variant (different gate format, CTA copy, or timing). Measure conversion to Foundation within 7 and 14 days. Use PostHog's built-in experiment analysis. Consider Statsig later if overlapping experiments need mutual exclusion layers or if peeking at results before statistical significance becomes a problem (PostHog doesn't do sequential testing; Statsig does).

5. How would you set up experimentation infrastructure?

PostHog is already loaded with session recording on, but feature flags are empty and autocapture is off. The tw-features array handles plan-based entitlements (static strings per tier, checked via features.includes()). PostHog experiments layer on top: they control how a gate is presented (hard wall vs blurred preview), not whether the feature is gated. First step: activate PostHog's experimentation module. Use posthog.getFeatureFlag() in React components, posthog.capture() for exposure and conversion events. Consistent hashing (MurmurHash on shop_id + flag_key) gives deterministic assignment at the shop level via PostHog's group analytics. If experiment volume grows past 10+ concurrent, evaluate Statsig for sequential testing (safe to peek at results without inflating false positives), mutual exclusion layers (prevent two experiments from confounding each other), and guardrail metrics (auto-flag when a winning variant harms retention or activation).

6. Tell me about a time you shipped a change that moved a business metric.

Kim Seybert, Shopify e-commerce. Brought in session replay tooling where none existed, instrumented the purchase funnel, ran CRO based on what the data showed. Conversion rates reached 234% above the e-commerce industry average. Revenue up 42% year-over-year. The approach: instrument first, watch sessions to understand why users drop off, test changes, measure. Same pipeline applies here: instrument PostHog, watch sessions (already running via PostHog + Clarity), experiment on gates, measure conversions.

7. How would you approach building growth surfaces around Moby?

Two gate designs, both producing PQL signals. (1) Answer truncation: Moby generates full responses via structured output with preview and full fields; free users see a truncated answer that ends at a natural data cliffhanger after a quantity-limited number of queries. (2) Time-boxed trial: free users get read-only Moby access for N days, controlled by a MOBY_TRIAL entitlement + moby_trial_expires_at on the subscription document in MongoDB. Both feed the PQL scoring pipeline via event aggregates from ClickHouse. See Moby Gates for architecture.

8. How do you work with product and design when you're the only engineer on a surface?

At Kim Seybert and Cosgrove/Barclays, I was the sole technical person interfacing with non-technical stakeholders (store owner, agency clients, account managers). The pattern: build a working version fast, put it in front of the stakeholder, iterate from their feedback. With Mantine as the design system, visual consistency comes from the component library, so the decisions are about flow, timing, and copy rather than pixels. For growth surfaces specifically, I'd propose variants grounded in data (session recordings, funnel analysis, PQL signals) and bring those to product for prioritization. The experiment framework makes this concrete: "I want to test variant A vs B on this gate; here's why, here's the conversion event, here's how long we need to run it."

9. Why Triple Whale?

Domain fit. E-commerce analytics is where my career has been, from the advertiser side (Victoria's Secret, Kim Seybert) to the agency side (Cosgrove/Barclays). The difference is first-party vs third-party: I've been the user of tools like Triple Whale, I know what the person on the other end of the growth surface needs. The PLG motion is unusual in this category; most competitors (Northbeam, Rockerbox, Polar) are sales-led. The role is building the growth function from scratch, which matches how I work best (Kim Seybert CRO from zero, Liberty Pest scheduling from zero). The AI adjacency through Moby is a genuine interest; my OSS contributions are to AI projects, and I'm the top external contributor on most of them.

10. What are you optimizing for in your next role?

A domain that makes intuitive sense (I've been on both sides of e-commerce analytics), impact I can see directly (growth metrics are unambiguous), and the autonomy of building a function from scratch as the first growth engineer. Soho hybrid works, and the team size means decisions happen fast.

Moby Gates

Two gate architectures for Moby on the free tier. Both follow the same pattern: an entitlement string in tw-features controls access, a PostHog flag controls the gate's presentation variant, the backend enforces the boundary, and the gate produces a PQL signal.

Gate A: Answer Truncation

Free users get a fixed number of Moby queries (e.g., 10). Moby answers each one, but the answers are truncated at a natural breakpoint. The user doesn't know answers are truncated until they see it. This creates a two-stage conversion funnel: the quantity limit creates scarcity (users spend their queries carefully), and the truncation creates desire for completeness once they see that Moby's partial answers are already valuable.

Structured output

The Moby system prompt for free-tier requests instructs the LLM to return structured output with two fields:

{
  "preview": "Your top 3 channels by ROAS: Google Shopping (4.2x),
              Meta Prospecting (3.1x), TikTok Spark Ads (2.8x).
              Two channels are underperforming relative to spend...",
  "full": "Your top 3 channels by ROAS: Google Shopping (4.2x),
           Meta Prospecting (3.1x), TikTok Spark Ads (2.8x).
           Two channels are underperforming relative to spend:
           Meta Retargeting is at 0.9x (below breakeven) and
           Google Performance Max dropped from 3.1x to 1.4x
           over the last 14 days. Recommended reallocation: ..."
}

The LLM already generates the full analysis via run_sql. Asking it to produce a natural-sounding cliffhanger alongside the full response is a prompt-level change, not infrastructure. The backend stores both; the API returns only preview for free-tier requests. No second model call, no added latency.

Entitlements and schema

  • MOBY_PREVIEW in tw-features
  • tw-subscription.moby_queries_remaining: number (decremented per query, initialized at signup)
  • API response shape: { preview: string, full: string | null, truncated: boolean, queries_remaining: number }
  • full is null for free-tier responses. The frontend never receives the complete answer.

Backend

The Moby tool-use router checks the shop's entitlement and query balance before executing. If MOBY_PREVIEW is present and queries_remaining > 0: run the query, decrement the counter, return the preview field only. If queries_remaining === 0: return a gate payload with upgrade CTA. The counter lives on the shop document in MongoDB, same as other subscription state.

React

function MobyResponse({ response }) {
  const posthog = usePostHog()

  if (response.truncated) {
    posthog.capture('moby_preview_shown', {
      queries_remaining: response.queries_remaining
    })
    return (
      <>
        <ChatBubble text={response.preview} />
        <UpgradeGate
          message={`See the full analysis (${response.queries_remaining} queries left)`}
          trigger="moby_truncation"
        />
      </>
    )
  }
  return <ChatBubble text={response.full} />
}

PostHog experiment

Flag moby-preview-depth, variants: 3-items (truncate after 3 data points), summary-only (one-line summary, no detail). The variant is passed to the system prompt so the LLM adjusts the preview's natural breakpoint accordingly. Conversion event: plan_upgraded within 14 days.

PQL signal

moby_previews_viewed_7d. Repeated engagement with truncated answers signals the user finds value but hits the ceiling. Weight: high. Combine with queries_remaining === 0 for the strongest signal.

Gate B: Time-Boxed Trial

Free users get full read-only Moby access (data questions via run_sql, no action tools) for N days after signup. After expiry, Moby responds with an upgrade gate. The trial is tied to the shop's domain, so creating new accounts doesn't reset it.

Entitlements and schema

  • MOBY_TRIAL in tw-features, added at signup
  • tw-subscription.moby_trial_expires_at: ISO timestamp
  • Backend checks both: entitlement present AND now < expires_at
  • After expiry: remove MOBY_TRIAL from the entitlement array, or leave it and let the timestamp gate

Backend

The Moby tool-use router already checks plan tier before executing tools. Add a branch: if MOBY_TRIAL present and not expired, allow read tools (run_sql, table_schema, sql_examples). Reject action tools (automations.draft_automation, Meta connector, GeoLift) regardless of trial status; those are Foundation/Automate entitlements. On expiry, return a gate payload with the upgrade CTA and a summary of what the user asked during the trial ("You asked Moby 14 questions about your ad performance. Upgrade to keep going.").

PostHog experiment

Flag moby-trial-duration, variants: 3d, 5d, 7d. Backend reads the variant at account creation via posthog.getFeatureFlag() (server-side SDK) and sets moby_trial_expires_at accordingly. Conversion event: plan_upgraded within 14 days of trial expiry. Exposure event: moby_trial_started.

PQL signal

moby_trial_query_count. High query count + expired trial + no upgrade = high-intent lead. Weight: very high.

Skill scoping

Moby skills are gated by plan tier at the tool-call layer. Free users can reach data-analysis (read-only SQL queries). Foundation unlocks Moby Specialists (media buying, creative, CRO agents). Automate unlocks automations-operator. Enterprise add-ons like Compass (Bayesian MMM, $4,800/mo, dedicated data science team) are gated separately. Users don't see skill names or routing; they type a question and Moby routes it. When a free user's question would route to a gated skill, Moby answers the diagnostic part (read tools allowed) and gates the action part: "I can tell you your Meta CPA increased 34% because your top creative fatigued. To adjust your budget allocation, upgrade to Foundation."
